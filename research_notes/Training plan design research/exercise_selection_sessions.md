# Exercise Selection, Exercise Order and Session Structure (Hypertrophy and Strength, Beginners to Intermediates)

Note on style: dashes are avoided on purpose (project rule). Source links follow each claim in brackets.

## Exercise selection: compounds vs isolations, movement patterns, joint angles, stretch-focused work, regional hypertrophy

### Takeaway
Build each session on 1 to 2 multi-joint lifts, then fill gaps with isolation work chosen to train muscles at long lengths (overhead triceps extension, seated leg curl, deep-stretch calf and leg extension positions). Different exercises for the same muscle grow it in different regions, so a muscle is best covered by 2 or more exercises with different joint angles across the week, not by one "best" exercise.

### Cited Findings
- Time-efficient training review (Iversen et al. 2021, Sports Medicine) recommends prioritising bilateral, multi-joint exercises and at least 4 weekly sets per muscle group in a 6 to 15 RM range as the minimum effective approach ([Iversen et al. 2021, PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34125411/))
- Overhead vs neutral arm cable triceps extension (Maeo et al., Eur J Sport Sci, online 2022): 12 weeks, 21 adults, one arm per condition. Overhead training produced substantially greater triceps hypertrophy, especially in the biarticular long head, despite lighter loads; reported long head gains of about +28.5% vs +19.6% ([PubMed 35819335](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35819335/); numbers via [Sci-Sport summary](https://sci-sport.com/en/triceps-hypertrophy-impact-of-arm-position/)). Caveat: an earlier study (Stasinaki et al. 2018) found no significant difference between positions ([Sci-Sport summary](https://sci-sport.com/en/triceps-hypertrophy-impact-of-arm-position/))
- Seated vs prone leg curl (Maeo et al. 2021, MSSE): 20 adults, 5 x 10 at 70% 1RM, 2x/week, 12 weeks, one leg per condition. Whole-hamstring volume grew about 14% seated vs about 9% prone; difference not significant for the short head (monoarticular). Authors: training at long muscle lengths promotes hypertrophy ([PubMed 33009197](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33009197/))
- Leg extension ROM (Pedrosa et al. 2022): in untrained women, lengthened partial ROM (100 to 65 degrees knee flexion) produced larger distal quadriceps hypertrophy than full ROM (100 to 30) or shortened partial ROM (65 to 30); 1RM gains were ROM specific ([SportRxiv preprint citing Pedrosa](https://sportrxiv.org/index.php/server/preprint/download/414/865/806); [train.fitness summary](https://train.fitness/personal-trainer-blogs/do-half-reps-build-as-much-muscle))
- Calf raise ROM (Kassiano et al. 2023): 42 young women, 8 weeks, 3 days/week; lengthened partials gave larger medial gastrocnemius thickness gains than full ROM or shortened partials ([PubMed 37015016](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37015016/))
- Meta-analysis of partial vs full ROM (Wolf et al. 2023, Int J Strength Cond): 23 studies, 8 on hypertrophy; small effect favouring lengthened partials over full ROM (ES 0.28) and trivial effect favouring full ROM over shortened partials (ES 0.08) ([IUSCA journal](https://journal.iusca.org/index.php/Journal/article/view/182))
- Debate: a 2025 within-participant study by Wolf and colleagues in trained people found lengthened partials gave similar adaptations to full ROM ([PeerJ 2025](https://peerj.com/articles/18904)); a 2024 meta-analysis (Varovic et al.) and a 2024 preprint (Carlson et al.) report trivial differences between long and short muscle length training and note underpowered studies ([summary of search results incl. SportRxiv](https://sportrxiv.org/index.php/server/preprint/download/485/1040/972))
- Kassiano et al. 2023 systematic review "Which ROMs lead to Rome?" examined ROM effects on hypertrophy ([PubMed 36662126](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36662126/))
- Regional quads: single-joint knee extension produces preferential rectus femoris hypertrophy, while leg press gives similar vastus lateralis growth; squat protocols have shown minimal rectus femoris growth ([SportRxiv 2024 hip-angle leg extension study, DOI 10.51224/SRXIV.407](https://doi.org/10.51224/SRXIV.407)). The same 2024 study recommends reduced hip flexion (more reclined seat) on leg extension to maximise rectus femoris growth in untrained men ([DOI 10.51224/SRXIV.407](https://doi.org/10.51224/SRXIV.407))
- Upper chest: Chaves et al. 2020 (Int J Exerc Sci, untrained men, 8 weeks): incline-only pressing grew the upper pectoralis more than flat-only or combined; mid and lower chest grew similarly regardless of angle. Commonly cited angle range 30 to 45 degrees. Evidence base is thin and some commentators are not convinced ([BarBend summary](https://barbend.com/upper-chest-pressing-wrong-angle); [Lyfta summary](https://www.lyfta.app/articles/incline-vs-flat-bench-press))
- Glutes: Plotkin et al. 2023 (Frontiers in Physiology, untrained, 9 weeks, MRI): hip thrust and back squat produced similar gluteus maximus growth (small non-significant edge to hip thrust); squat produced more quadriceps and adductor growth; hip thrust had higher EMG but EMG did not predict hypertrophy ([PubMed 37877099](https://www.pubmed.gov/37877099/))

### Inferences
- Rule for an AI coach: for each main muscle, prefer at least one exercise per week that loads it at long length (stretch under load): overhead or cable overhead triceps extension, seated leg curl, incline dumbbell curl or similar for biceps, deep leg press or leg extension with reclined back, standing or leg-press calf raise with full stretch, Romanian deadlift for hamstrings.
- Multi-joint lifts do not fully cover all regions: squat/leg press under-trains rectus femoris (add leg extension), hip hinges and squats under-train the hamstrings' knee-flexion function (add a leg curl, ideally seated), presses under-train the triceps long head (add an overhead extension).
- Squats give "more muscle per exercise" than hip thrusts for lower body (quads plus adductors plus glutes); a hip thrust is a glute-focused add-on, not a squat replacement.
- When a catalog tags each exercise with one main muscle, the coach should treat compounds as also training secondary muscles (bench: triceps, front delts; rows/pulldowns: biceps; squats: glutes, adductors) to avoid double-counting or overloading small muscles.
- Lengthened partials are a reasonable optional technique (for example on the last set of an isolation) but should not be a default since newer data show parity with full ROM.

### Gaps
- No controlled study found here for lat vs upper back regional hypertrophy (e.g. pulldown vs row, grip width); guidance there is mostly anatomical/EMG reasoning.
- Kassiano 2023 calf study: equipment (leg press vs Smith) and exact gain figures were not confirmed.
- Chaves study read only through secondary sources.

## Movement-pattern coverage per week and push vs pull balance

### Takeaway
No research establishes an optimal weekly pattern mix or push/pull ratio. A practical template is to cover horizontal push, horizontal pull, vertical push, vertical pull, squat or knee-dominant, hinge, single-leg/lunge and knee flexion every week, with pulling volume at least equal to pushing volume (many coaches go up to 2:1, horizontal pulling emphasised).

### Cited Findings
- ACSM 2009 position stand recommends programs include multiple-joint and single-joint exercises and sequences them large before small; applies across total-body, upper/lower and body-part splits ([ACSM 2009, PubMed 19204579](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19204579/); [full text PDF](https://sfu.ca/~ryand/kin343/ACSMresistance.pdf))
- Practitioner ratios vary: one coach recommends 3:1 pull:push by reps and 2:1 horizontal:vertical pulling; Muscle & Strength suggests 2:1; another practitioner blog suggests at least 1:1 or slightly favour pulling (exact attribution of the 1:1 claim among these practitioner pages was not verified) ([Bodybuilding.com](https://bodybuilding.com/content/safe-strong-upper-body-lifting-begins-with-these-two-ratios.html); [Muscle & Strength](https://www.muscleandstrength.com/articles/no-pain-shoulder-superset); [practitioner blog](https://ryan-magada-h8kq.squarespace.com/blog-1/2025/2/18/push-vs-pull-exercises-why-your-workout-needs-the-right-push-pull-ratio); [Breaking Muscle](https://breakingmuscle.com/advanced-programming-principles-for-shoulder-and-knee-health/))
- Measured population norms (dynamometer) show a pull:push ratio near 1:1.12; these are descriptive norms, not evidence that a ratio prevents injury ([McKean presentation](https://www.slideshare.net/markmckean96/shoulder-strength-ratios-stability-rom-and-muscle-balance))
- Coaches recommend including rotator cuff and scapular work such as face pulls ([Muscle & Strength](https://www.muscleandstrength.com/articles/no-pain-shoulder-superset))
- Squat and hip thrust train different muscles beyond the glutes (squat adds quads and adductors), illustrating that patterns are not interchangeable ([Plotkin 2023](https://www.pubmed.gov/37877099/))
- Seated leg curl (knee flexion at long hamstring length) grew hamstrings more than prone curl, supporting a dedicated knee flexion pattern ([Maeo 2021](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33009197/))

### Inferences
- Suggested weekly minimum for a general gym-goer: 1 or more exercises per pattern among horizontal push (bench, push-up, chest press), horizontal pull (row variants), vertical push (overhead press), vertical pull (pulldown, pull-up), squat/knee dominant (squat, leg press, hack squat), hinge (RDL, deadlift, hip thrust), lunge/single leg (split squat, lunge, step-up) and knee flexion (leg curl).
- Push vs pull rule: weekly hard sets for pulling (rows plus pulldowns plus rear delts) should be greater than or equal to pressing sets. If a session has 2 presses, include at least 2 pulls.
- In a catalog with one main muscle per exercise, map patterns via muscle: chest = horizontal push, shoulders = vertical push, lats/back = pulls, quads = squat/lunge, hamstrings = hinge and knee flexion, glutes = hinge/hip thrust.

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed study found that sets a push:pull ratio for hypertrophy or shoulder health; recommendations above are expert opinion.

## Exercise variation vs consistency

### Takeaway
Some systematic variation helps (different exercises reach different regions), but random or very frequent rotation reduces gains, especially strength on lifts that are not practised. Keep main lifts stable for a block (roughly 4 to 8+ weeks) so progress can be tracked; rotate accessories more freely if motivation needs it.

### Cited Findings
- Kassiano et al. 2022 systematic review (JSCR 36(6):1753-1762; 8 studies, 241 young men): some systematic variation seems to enhance regional hypertrophy and maximise dynamic strength, while excessive, random variation and exercises that provide redundant stimuli may compromise gains ([PubMed 35438660](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35438660/); [Biolayne summary](https://biolayne.com/reps/issue-7/exercise-variation-for-strength-and-hypertrophy-change-is-not-always-good/))
- Baz-Valle et al. 2019 (PLoS ONE; 19 trained men completed; 8 weeks, 4 sessions/week): fixed exercises group vs random exercises each session picked by an app from 80 exercises, volume equated. Vastus lateralis thickness +0.18 cm fixed vs +0.12 cm random; rectus femoris +0.18 vs +0.05; vastus intermedius increased only in the fixed group; no significant between-group differences. Bench 1RM +7.1 kg fixed vs +4.0 kg random (relative +4.7% vs +0.77%), squat +11.9 vs +10.2 kg. Random variation improved intrinsic motivation (ES 1.28). Authors: limit variety to maximise growth and strength and keep complex free-weight lifts in regular rotation ([PMC6934277](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6934277/))
- Strength gains are specific to the trained exercise and ROM: Pedrosa leg extension 1RM gains were ROM specific ([train.fitness summary](https://train.fitness/personal-trainer-blogs/do-half-reps-build-as-much-muscle)); free-weight training improves free-weight tests more than machine training ([Haugen 2023](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC10426227/)); squat vs hip thrust each improved their own lift more ([Plotkin 2023](https://www.pubmed.gov/37877099/))

### Inferences
- Rule: keep the first 1 to 2 lifts of each session (the tracked lifts) identical for at least one training block; swap only when progress stalls for several sessions, pain occurs, or equipment is missing.
- Accessories can rotate between blocks, or on request for enjoyment, but rotate to a meaningfully different angle/length (e.g. pushdown to overhead extension), not a redundant near-duplicate.
- Do not randomise exercises session to session for users who want strength on a specific lift.
- If a user reports boredom, variation is a legitimate tool for adherence (motivation effect in Baz-Valle).

### Gaps
- No direct study found that pins an optimal rotation interval (e.g. 4 vs 8 vs 12 weeks); block length here is an inference.

## Exercise order within a session

### Takeaway
Order mainly affects strength (and performance) on whichever exercise comes first; for hypertrophy order matters little when volume is equated. Default: multi-joint before single-joint, large before small, but put the user's priority muscle or lift first.

### Cited Findings
- Nunes et al. 2021 meta-analysis (Eur J Sport Sci; 11 studies): strength gains overall similar across orders, but for multi-joint exercises gains favoured doing them first (ES 0.32, p = 0.034); hypertrophy showed no order effect (ES 0.03, p = 0.862). Authors: strength improves more in exercises performed at the beginning of the session, so apply specificity ([PubMed 32077380](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32077380/); [searchworks record](https://searchworks.jioinstitute.edu.in/articles/cmedm__32077380))
- Limitation: in most order studies, hypertrophy was measured in the muscle that was prime mover in the single-joint exercise but only a synergist in the compound ([Examine](https://examine.com/conditions/muscle-size-and-strength/faq/which-exercise-should-be-performed-first-in-a-workout))
- ACSM 2009: sequence large muscle group before small, multiple-joint before single-joint, to preserve exercise intensity; evidence category C ([ACSM 2009](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19204579/); [PDF](https://sfu.ca/~ryand/kin343/ACSMresistance.pdf))
- Simão et al. 2012 review argues order should not always follow large-before-small and that client needs or movement patterns needing improvement should determine order ([IDEA Fit summary](https://www.ideafit.com/does-exercise-order-really-matter-in-resistance-training))
- Brandão et al. 2020 (JSCR) compared orders of single- and multi-joint upper body exercises; a secondary summary reports slightly better strength when starting with compounds and minimal hypertrophy differences ([PubMed 32149887](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32149887/))
- Coaching sources: train a lagging muscle earlier since focus and performance drift downward over a session (opinion, not research) ([Outlift](https://outlift.com/exercise-order-for-hypertrophy))

### Inferences
- Ordering algorithm for one session:
  1. Priority lift or lagging muscle exercise (if the user set one), otherwise
  2. Heaviest, most technical multi-joint lift (squat, deadlift, bench, overhead press),
  3. Second multi-joint lift for another pattern (e.g. row after bench, RDL after squat),
  4. Machine or dumbbell compounds,
  5. Isolations for muscles not yet trained directly, long-length variants preferred,
  6. Small muscles that assist earlier lifts last (triceps after presses, biceps after pulls, calves, abs, rear delts).
- Avoid pre-fatiguing a synergist before its compound unless intended: e.g. do not place hard triceps work before bench press, or leg curls before heavy deadlifts.
- Place the most technical/axial-loading lifts early when the user is freshest, for safety and performance (inference from ACSM intensity-preservation rationale).

### Gaps
- Few longitudinal studies on "priority principle" for hypertrophy; direct evidence that putting a lagging muscle first grows it more is lacking.

## Supersets, other time savers and rest intervals

### Takeaway
Antagonist (or unrelated muscle) supersets cut session time by roughly 35 to 40% with similar hypertrophy and strength, at the cost of higher perceived exertion. Same-muscle (agonist) supersets reduce volume load and are best avoided except as an intentional intensity technique. Heavy compounds should get longer rest (2 to 3+ minutes) and generally not be supersetted; isolation work tolerates 60 to 90 seconds.

### Cited Findings
- Zhang, Weakley, Li, Li, Garcia-Ramos 2025 meta-analysis (Sports Medicine 55(4):953-975; 19 studies, 313 participants, only 3 chronic): sessions about 37% shorter; training efficiency SMD 1.74; overall volume load no difference (SMD 0.05); agonist-antagonist supersets gave more total reps (SMD 0.68) and a non-significant volume load increase (SMD 0.53); similar-biomechanics (same muscle) supersets lowered volume load (SMD -1.08); hypertrophy similar (SMD -0.05), 1RM similar (SMD 0.10); RPE higher (SMD 0.77); blood lactate higher; CK difference not significant. Authors recommend agonist-antagonist supersets when time is limited, avoiding similar-biomechanics supersets unless near-failure training is the goal, and allowing longer recovery after superset sessions ([PMC12011898](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12011898/); [digibug abstract](https://digibug.ugr.es/handle/10481/102755))
- Burke, Hermann, Wolf, Schoenfeld et al. 2025 (Science & Sports; preprint "Less time, same gains"): 43 trained adults; superset sessions about 36% shorter with similar muscle growth ([RGU repository](https://rgu-repository.worktribe.com/output/2934908/less-time-same-gains-comparison-of-superset-vs-traditional-set-training-on-muscular-adaptations); [SportRxiv](https://sportrxiv.org/index.php/server/preprint/view/419)); press reports about 44 vs 69 minutes per workout and more discomfort, including nausea, in the superset group ([Men's Health AU](https://menshealth.com.au/?p=69211); [T3](https://www.t3.com/active/short-on-time-a-study-reveals-this-workout-hack-helps-you-gain-more-muscle-in-40-percent-less-time))
- Weakley et al. 2017: crossover of traditional, superset and tri-set structures; supersets and tri-sets were more efficient, raised lactate and perceived exertion, and reduced countermovement jump at 24 h ([Leeds Beckett eprint](https://eprints.leedsbeckett.ac.uk/id/eprint/3890); [ACU](https://acuresearchbank.acu.edu.au/item/8629y/the-effects-of-traditional-superset-and-tri-set-resistance-training-structures-on-perceived-intensity-and-physiological-responses)). Reported durations 42.3 vs 24.0 vs 17.7 min come from a secondary summary, not verified ([Biolayne](https://biolayne.com/articles/training/science-backed-supersets/))
- Iversen et al. 2021: supersets, drop sets and rest-pause roughly halve training time while keeping volume similar; possibly better suited to hypertrophy than maximal strength ([PubMed 34125411](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34125411/))
- Andersen et al. 2022: superset sessions were more time-efficient but reduced volume and felt harder than traditional sessions ([Frontiers in Psychology 2022](https://frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2022.912368/pdf))
- Antagonist pre-loading: performance improved when the antagonist exercise was done immediately, 30 s or 1 min before the agonist set, but not with longer gaps (de Freitas Maia et al., via secondary summary) ([Breaking Muscle](https://breakingmuscle.com/antagonist-pairing-shows-great-promise/))
- Rest intervals, Singer et al. 2024 Bayesian meta-analysis (Frontiers in Sports and Active Living; 9 studies, 19 measurements): small hypertrophy benefit for rests over 60 s; no appreciable extra benefit beyond 90 s; effects favoured longer rest for arm (0.13) and thigh (0.17) with credible intervals crossing zero; training to failure did not modify the rest effect; mostly untrained participants ([Frontiers abstract](https://frontiersin.org/journals/sports-and-active-living/articles/10.3389/fspor.2024.1429789/abstract))
- Schoenfeld et al. 2016 (JSCR; trained men, 8 weeks): 3 min rest produced greater squat and bench 1RM gains than 1 min, and some hypertrophy measures favoured 3 min; attributed largely to more reps/volume in later sets ([PubMed 26605807](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26605807/))

### Inferences
- Superset pairing rules:
  - Good (antagonist or non-competing): bench press + row (with moderate loads), incline dumbbell press + pulldown, overhead press + chin-up/pulldown, biceps curl + triceps extension, leg extension + leg curl, lateral raise + leg curl, calf raise + abs, face pull + push-up.
  - Upper/lower pairings with small lower-body moves (e.g. lateral raise + calf raise) also work.
  - Avoid: same muscle pairs (e.g. bench + flyes, pulldown + row both loading lats heavily, squat + leg press) unless deliberately used as an intensity technique on the last exercise; any pairing involving heavy squats, deadlifts or other heavy axial lifts (fatigue, breathing, technique risk; also needs 2 to 3+ min rest); pairings that need two distant stations in a busy gym; pairing grip-intensive pulls with deadlifts.
  - Do not superset the session's tracked main lift if the user prioritises strength on it; superset accessories instead.
  - Beginners: expect higher RPE; limit supersets to accessories.
- Rest rule of thumb: heavy compounds 2 to 3 minutes (strength focus 3+ minutes); machine compounds about 2 minutes; isolations 60 to 90 seconds; within an antagonist superset, roughly 1 to 2 min after completing the pair.

### Gaps
- Chronic superset hypertrophy data are few (2 to 3 studies in the meta-analysis); most participants young trained males.
- Singer 2024 did not separately analyse compound vs isolation exercises; the "longer rest for compounds" rule is an inference plus Schoenfeld 2016 and ACSM guidance.

## Session length and number of exercises per session

### Takeaway
Little direct evidence fixes an ideal session length. Practical target: 4 to 8 exercises in 45 to 75 minutes; supersets let 6 to 8 exercises fit into under an hour.

### Cited Findings
- Traditional set sessions in a trained-lifter trial lasted about 69 minutes vs about 44 minutes with supersets for the same programme ([Men's Health AU summary of Burke et al.](https://menshealth.com.au/?p=69211))
- Baz-Valle 2019 used 6 exercises x 3 sets per session, 4 sessions per week, as a workable trained-lifter session ([PMC6934277](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6934277/))
- Minimum effective dose: at least 4 weekly sets per muscle, multi-joint emphasis ([Iversen 2021](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34125411/))
- Supersets cut session time by about 37% ([Zhang 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12011898/))

### Inferences
- Time budget estimate: a straight set takes about 2 to 4 minutes including rest (set ~40 s plus 1.5 to 3 min rest), so 3 sets of one exercise is about 7 to 12 minutes. A 60 minute session therefore fits roughly 5 to 7 exercises at 3 sets each with straight sets, or 7 to 9 with antagonist supersets.
- Rule: if the user's session time is short (under 45 min), prefer fewer exercises with compounds, and superset the accessories.

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed source found establishing an optimal session duration or maximum number of exercises for hypertrophy.

## Warm-ups before heavy lifts

### Takeaway
Use a short general warm-up, then 1 to 3 progressive specific warm-up sets before the first heavy compound, with the last set around 80% of working load; later exercises for already warmed muscles need 0 to 1 warm-up sets.

### Cited Findings
- Ribeiro et al. 2014: a specific warm-up of 1 set x 10 reps at 50% of test load did not change total reps or fatigue index versus no warm-up across 4 sets at 80% 1RM of bench, squat and curl ([Scribd copy of paper](https://www.scribd.com/document/362423660/ribeiro2014-aquecimen-pdf))
- Ribeiro et al. 2020: 1 warm-up set (6 reps at 80% of training load) improved squat force output; 2 progressive sets (6 at 40%, 6 at 80%) improved bench press; warming up with few reps and low loads alone was not enough to optimise performance ([IntechOpen chapter summarising](https://www.intechopen.com/online-first/75109); [J Men's Health 2021](https://doaj.org/article/90de36e35e514c36af5f61118a42eb3a))
- Ribeiro group 2021: specific warm-up improved first-set velocity for bench press and squat at 80% 1RM ([J Men's Health 2021](https://libmast.utm.my/Record/doaj.art-90de36e35e514c36af5f61118a42eb3a))
- Unclear whether benefit comes from loads or number of sets ([SportRxiv preprint](https://sportrxiv.org/index.php/server/preprint/view/559))

### Inferences
- Rule: first heavy compound of the session: 2 to 3 ramp sets (e.g. bar or about 40 to 50% x 8, about 60 to 70% x 5, about 80% x 2 to 3); a second compound for different muscles: 1 to 2 ramp sets; isolations and machines after the muscles are warm: 0 to 1 light set. Warm-up sets should be low rep and not fatiguing and should not count as working sets.

### Gaps
- No study found that defines an optimal number of warm-up sets; the ramp scheme above is an inference.

## Machines vs free weights for hypertrophy

### Takeaway
For hypertrophy machines and free weights are equivalent; strength gains are specific to the modality trained. An AI coach can freely substitute machines for free weights for growth, but should keep the tested lift in the program if the user wants strength on it.

### Cited Findings
- Haugen et al. 2023 meta-analysis (BMC Sports Sci Med Rehabil; 13 studies, 1,016 adults): hypertrophy similar (SMD -0.055, p = 0.751; only 5 studies); free-weight strength tests improved more with free-weight training (SMD -0.210, p = 0.023); machine tests trended toward machine training; jump height similar (free weights superior after one study removed); authors say choice comes down to preference and goals ([PMC10426227](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC10426227/))
- Stronger by Science commentary agrees with these findings ([Stronger by Science](https://www.strongerbyscience.com/free-weights-vs-machines/))
- Baz-Valle et al. recommend keeping complex free-weight lifts in rotation for strength ([PMC6934277](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6934277/))

### Inferences
- Machines are a good default for beginners and for isolations and long-length positions (cable overhead extension, seated leg curl), and are easy to superset and to progress in small increments.
- Combining modalities is reasonable; machines are especially useful late in a session when fatigue makes free-weight technique less reliable.

### Gaps
- Hypertrophy comparison rests on only 5 studies; long-term trained-lifter data are sparse.
